# Custom Mailer Boxes for E-commerce: A Buyer's Guide

For an online brand, the shipping box is often the first physical thing a customer touches. It has two jobs at once: get the product there undamaged, and make the moment of opening feel like part of the brand. Custom mailer boxes are built to do both. This guide walks through what to decide before you request a quote, so you get a box that protects your product, fits your budget and looks the way you intended.

## Why branded mailer boxes matter

A plain brown carton does the protective job, but it says nothing about who sent it. A printed mailer box turns delivery into a branded experience: your logo is visible on the doorstep, the reveal inside can carry a message or pattern, and a well-fitted box signals care. Customers are more likely to share an unboxing they enjoyed, which gives your packaging a second life as content.

A custom size also has practical benefits. A box made to your product's dimensions needs less void fill, takes less space in a courier vehicle and may reduce dimensional-weight charges. The exact savings depend on your carrier and product mix, so it is worth checking your own shipping invoices before and after switching.

## Choosing a mailer box style

The most common styles for e-commerce are:

- **Tuck-front mailer (roll end front tuck).** The classic subscription-box style. It folds from one flat piece, closes without tape and opens like a lid. Good for apparel, cosmetics, accessories and small electronics.
- **Regular slotted carton (RSC).** The standard four-flap shipping carton. Economical and strong, best for heavier or bulk shipments where presentation is less important.
- **Die-cut boxes with inserts.** When products need to stay in place, a die-cut structure with a paperboard or molded pulp insert holds each item securely.
- **Tear-strip and return-ready boxes.** A tear strip makes opening easy; some designs add a second adhesive strip so the customer can reuse the box for a return. Availability of specific features at your quantity: TBD in quote.

If you are not sure which structure suits your product, send the product (or its dimensions and weight) to the manufacturer and ask for a structural proposal and dieline.

## Board and flute: building for transit

Corrugated board is made of liners and a fluted middle layer. The flute size and number of walls determine how much the box resists crushing and impact.

- **Fine flutes (such as E-flute)** give a smooth printing surface and a slim, premium feel, and suit lighter products.
- **Larger flutes (such as B- or C-flute)** add cushioning and stacking strength.
- **Double wall** combinations are used for heavier or fragile goods.

The right choice depends on product weight, fragility, how the parcel travels (courier, pallet, international freight) and how the box will be stacked. A good supplier will recommend a board grade for your situation rather than a one-size-fits-all spec. Liner color is a design choice too. Kraft liners give a natural, eco-leaning look; white liners make printed colors brighter.

## Printing options

How you print affects the look, the cost and the minimum quantity.

- **Flexographic printing** prints directly on corrugated board. It is cost-effective for simple logos and one- to few-color designs at larger runs.
- **Digital printing** works well for full-color artwork, short runs and multiple design versions, since no printing plates are needed.
- **Litho-lamination** prints artwork on paper by offset, then laminates it onto the corrugated board. It gives the sharpest images and richest color, suited to premium retail or gift mailers.

**Inside printing** is one of the most effective ways to make an unboxing memorable: a pattern, a thank-you message or a QR code to your store. It adds a printing pass, so ask how it changes price and lead time (TBD per quote).

Finishes such as matte or gloss varnish, lamination and spot effects can also be added. Keep in mind that each extra process can add cost and time.

## Getting the size right

Measure the product, including any inner packaging, and decide how much clearance or cushioning you need. Always tell the supplier whether your dimensions are **internal** (the space inside the box) or **external**, and give them as length × width × depth. If you ship several products in different combinations, consider a small set of box sizes that covers most orders, rather than a different box for every SKU.

When in doubt, send a physical product sample. The factory can build a white (unprinted) sample to check fit before artwork is final.

## Sustainability without overclaiming

Corrugated board is widely recycled, and kraft or recycled-content board, water-based inks and plastic-free inserts such as molded pulp can all reduce your packaging's footprint. Be careful with wording on the box itself: claims like "100% recycled", "FSC certified" or "compostable" must be backed by documents for that specific material. ## The ordering process

A typical custom mailer project runs in five steps:

1. **Brief.** Product dimensions and weight, estimated quantity, artwork or references, destination country.
2. **Quote and structure.** The supplier proposes style, board, printing method and a dieline.
3. **Proof and sample.** A digital proof for artwork placement, then a physical sample to check fit and color. Sample lead time: TBD.
4. **Production and quality control.** Mass production with inspection before packing. Production lead time: TBD.
5. **Shipping.** Export packing and documentation. Trade terms (for example FOB or CIF) are agreed in the quote.

## Questions to ask your supplier

- Which board grade do you recommend for my product weight and shipping route, and why?
- Which printing method fits my artwork and quantity?
- What does inside printing add to price and lead time?
- Can I approve a physical sample before production?
- How is color checked during production?
- How will the boxes be packed for export, and what documents are provided?

## Cost drivers to understand

Without quoting numbers, the main factors that move price are: box size and board grade, printing method and number of colors, inside printing and finishes, inserts, order quantity, and shipping method. Asking for quotes at two or three quantities is the easiest way to see how these interact for your project.

## Summary

The best mailer box is the one that fits your product snugly, survives your shipping route and carries your brand consistently. Decide on style, board, printing and size, verify any sustainability claims, and approve a sample before production.
