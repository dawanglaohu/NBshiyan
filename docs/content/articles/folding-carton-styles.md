# Folding Carton Styles Explained: Tuck End, Auto Bottom, Sleeve and Window

Folding cartons are the printed paperboard boxes you see on retail shelves: cosmetics, tea, supplements, small electronics, candles. They ship flat, are folded and glued into shape, and carry most of a product's branding. The structure you choose affects how the box is packed, how it holds the product and how it looks on the shelf. This guide explains the common styles and how to decide between them.

## Tuck end boxes

A tuck end box closes with flaps that tuck into the box at the top and bottom. It is the most common folding carton style.

- **Reverse tuck end (RTE):** the top flap tucks from the front and the bottom flap from the back. The panels are laid out efficiently on the sheet, which makes it a common choice for light products.
- **Straight tuck end (STE):** both flaps tuck from the same side, so the front panel stays clean at both ends. It is often preferred for premium looks and window boxes.
- **Tuck top with snap-lock bottom:** the top tucks in, while the bottom folds in four parts that interlock when pushed together. It holds more weight than a tucked bottom.

Tuck ends suit light to medium products that are packed by hand.

## Auto bottom (crash-lock) boxes

An auto bottom box, also called a crash-lock box, has a bottom that is pre-glued. When the flat box is opened, the bottom locks into place by itself.

- **Faster packing:** one motion to open, no bottom flaps to fold.
- **Stronger base:** better for heavier items such as jars and bottles.
- **Trade-off:** the extra gluing makes the box slightly more complex to produce than a simple tuck end.

### Tuck end vs auto bottom box: how to choose

| Question | Tuck end | Auto bottom |
|---|---|---|
| Product weight | Light | Medium to heavier |
| Packing speed | Slower (fold both ends) | Faster (bottom locks itself) |
| Bottom strength | Lower | Higher |
| Structure | Simpler | More gluing |

If the product is light and packed in small batches, a tuck end is usually enough. If it is heavy or packed in large numbers, an auto bottom often saves time and reduces failures.

## Sleeves

A sleeve is an open-ended tube of board that slides over a tray, a box or the product itself. Sleeves are a simple way to add branding or a seasonal design over a standard inner pack, and they pair well with drawer-style trays.

## Window boxes

A window box has a die-cut opening, often covered with a clear film, so the customer can see the product. Typical uses are cosmetics, food gifts and toys.

- Keep the window away from folds and glue areas so the box stays strong.
- Decide whether the window needs a film or can stay open.
- Plan the artwork around the window, because part of the panel will not be printed.

Window boxes custom-made for your product also need the insert to hold the product in the right spot behind the window.

## Board grades in general terms

Folding cartons are made from paperboard, not corrugated board. In general terms:

- **Coated white board** gives a smooth, bright surface for sharp printing.
- **Board with a white top and a grey or brown back** is a common, cost-effective option when the inside is not seen.
- **Kraft board** has a natural brown look; colours print warmer and darker on it.
- **Thicker board** feels sturdier but is harder to fold neatly on small boxes.

## Folding carton or shipping box?

A folding carton is a retail pack, not a shipping container. For courier delivery, cartons usually travel inside an outer corrugated box; see [custom corrugated boxes](/en/products/custom-corrugated-boxes) for that part of the pack.

## Summary

Use a tuck end for light products packed by hand, an auto bottom for heavier products or faster packing, a sleeve to brand an existing pack, and a window when the product should be seen. Choose the board together with the structure and the finish.
