# CMYK vs Pantone for Packaging: How to Choose

Your logo looks right on screen, then the first printed box comes back a little duller, a little warmer or simply different. In most cases the reason is not a mistake but the colour system. Packaging can be printed with CMYK process colours, with Pantone spot colours, or with a mix of both. Knowing how each works helps you choose before the artwork is final, not after the first proof.

## How CMYK works

CMYK stands for cyan, magenta, yellow and key (black). These four process inks are printed as tiny dots that overlap; the eye mixes them into the full range of colours you see in photos and gradients.

- **Strengths:** photos, illustrations, gradients and many colours in one design at no extra ink cost per colour.
- **Limits:** the CMYK gamut is smaller than what a screen can show. Bright oranges, vivid greens, deep blues, fluorescent and metallic colours often cannot be matched exactly.
- **Consistency:** because each colour is built from four inks, small shifts in ink density or paper can change the result between runs.

## How Pantone (spot) colours work

A spot colour is an ink mixed to one specific colour before printing, rather than built from dots. The Pantone Matching System is the most widely used reference for naming and communicating these colours, so a designer and a printer can refer to the same numbered swatch.

- **Strengths:** solid, even areas of colour; colours outside the CMYK gamut; more consistent brand colours across runs.
- **Limits:** each spot colour is a separate ink and printing unit, so it adds set-up work. How spot colours are priced: {{待Ryan确认}}.
- **Swatch books:** coated (C) and uncoated (U) versions of the same Pantone number look different, because paper affects colour. Choose the swatch that matches your actual stock.

## When to choose which

| Situation | Usually |
|---|---|
| Photos, illustrations, many colours | CMYK |
| A single strong brand colour on large areas | Pantone spot colour |
| Brand colour plus product photos | CMYK + one or two spot colours |
| Colours outside the CMYK gamut (bright orange, neon, metallic) | Spot colour or special ink |
| Several products that must match on the shelf | Spot colour for the key colour |

If your brand guide lists both CMYK and Pantone values, ask which one is the master. A CMYK conversion of a Pantone colour is an approximation, not the same colour.

## Paper, coating and finish change colour

The same ink looks different on coated paper, uncoated board and kraft. Brown kraft shifts every colour warmer and darker; uncoated stock absorbs ink and makes colours softer. Lamination and varnish change gloss and can slightly shift how colour reads. Decide on the material and finish first, then approve colour on that material.

## Colour proof types

A proof is how you approve colour before production. Common types in the industry:

- **Digital (PDF) proof:** checks layout, text and position. It does not show true colour, because every screen is different.
- **Hard-copy colour proof:** a printed proof from a calibrated proofing printer, used as a colour reference.
- **Drawdown:** a sample of a mixed spot ink on the actual paper, for checking a Pantone colour.
- **Press proof or production sample:** printed on the production process and material, the closest to the final result.

## How colour is checked and accepted

No printing process reproduces a colour perfectly every time, so agree in advance how colour will be judged: against which reference, under what light, and what difference is acceptable. ## Preparing your files

- Set the colour mode the printer asks for and name spot colours exactly as in the Pantone guide.
- Do not leave RGB images in a CMYK job; convert them and check the result.
- Send the reference: Pantone numbers with C or U, or a physical sample you want to match.

## Summary

Use CMYK for photos and multi-colour designs, add a spot colour when one brand colour has to be bright, solid and consistent, and approve colour on a proof printed on your real material. Decide this before the artwork is final.
