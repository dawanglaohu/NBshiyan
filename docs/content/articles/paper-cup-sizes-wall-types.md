# Paper Cup Sizes and Wall Types: A Guide for Coffee Shops

Choosing paper cups looks simple until you have to match sizes to your menu, pick a wall type that keeps hands comfortable and find lids that fit. This guide covers the common sizes, the difference between single, double and ripple wall cups, and what to check before you order branded cups.

## Paper cup sizes: oz chart

Cup sizes are usually named in fluid ounces (oz) in the US and many international chains, and in millilitres in Russia and much of Europe. The figures below are common industry conventions; the exact capacity of a cup varies by manufacturer and by how full you pour.

| Nominal size | Approx. capacity | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 4 oz | ~120 ml | Espresso, tasting samples |
| 8 oz | ~240 ml | Small cappuccino, flat white, tea |
| 12 oz | ~350 ml | Regular latte or americano |
| 16 oz | ~470 ml | Large coffee, iced drinks |
| 20 oz | ~590 ml | Extra-large and cold drinks |

Most coffee shops build their menu on three sizes, for example 8, 12 and 16 oz. Fewer sizes mean fewer lid types and simpler stock.

## Rim diameter and lids

Lids are matched to the rim diameter, not to the capacity. Two cups with the same capacity can have different rims, and one lid size often fits several cup sizes from the same series. Always check the lid against the actual cup before you order large quantities.

- **Sip lids** for hot drinks
- **Flat or dome lids** for cold drinks and drinks with toppings
- **Lids with a straw slot** for iced drinks

## Single vs double wall cups

### Single wall

One layer of paperboard. Light, compact to store and the simplest option.

- **Good for:** cold drinks, and hot drinks served with a sleeve or drunk quickly.
- **Watch out for:** hot drinks can make the cup uncomfortable to hold without a sleeve.

### Double wall

A second paper layer wrapped around the inner cup, with a small air gap between them.

- **Good for:** hot drinks without a separate sleeve; feels sturdier in the hand.
- **Trade-off:** takes more storage space and costs more per cup than single wall.

### Ripple wall

An outer layer with a corrugated, ribbed texture. The ridges add grip and insulation.

- **Good for:** takeaway hot drinks and a distinctive look.
- **Trade-off:** the textured surface affects how artwork looks; fine details and small text print less sharply than on a smooth wall.

### Single vs double wall cup: quick comparison

| | Single wall | Double wall | Ripple wall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hand comfort with hot drinks | Needs a sleeve | Good | Good |
| Storage space | Least | More | Most |
| Print surface | Smooth | Smooth | Textured |
| Typical use | Cold drinks, hot with sleeve | Hot drinks | Hot takeaway drinks |

## Hot vs cold drinks

Hot and cold drinks put different demands on a cup. Hot drinks need insulation for the hand; cold drinks create condensation on the outside, which can soften the board and smudge some print finishes. ## Printing your brand on cups

- Keep the logo away from the seam, where the artwork meets.
- Remember that the cup is a cone: straight artwork has to be distorted to look straight on the cup. Ask the manufacturer for the cup template.
- Strong, simple designs read better from a distance than detailed ones.
