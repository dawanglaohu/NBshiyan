# Corporate Gift Packaging: A Planning Guide for Brands and Buyers

A corporate gift is judged before it is opened. The box a client or employee receives says as much about your company as the item inside, and a well-made custom gift box is often kept and reused long after the contents are gone. This guide covers how to plan corporate gift packaging: choosing a box style, designing inserts, applying your brand, and building a timeline that gets everything delivered on time.

## Start with the brief

Before talking to a manufacturer, answer a few questions:

- **What goes inside?** A single item, a curated set, or a mix of products with different shapes? Collect dimensions and weights for every item.
- **Who receives it?** Clients, partners, employees, event guests? This affects the level of finish and the budget per box.
- **How will it be delivered?** Hand-delivered at an event, shipped to individual addresses, or delivered in bulk to offices? Individual shipping needs an outer shipping carton.
- **When must it arrive?** Work backwards from this date (see the timeline section).
- **How many?** Quantity affects which structures and finishes make sense, and the price per unit.

A clear brief lets the manufacturer propose the right structure on the first round, saving time.

## Choosing a rigid gift box style

Rigid boxes are built from a thick greyboard core wrapped in printed or specialty paper. They feel substantial and do not fold flat in the customer's hands. Common styles:

- **Magnetic closure box.** A hinged lid that closes with hidden magnets. Elegant opening, easy to reuse. A popular choice for premium corporate sets.
- **Lid and base (telescope) box.** A separate lid lifted off a base. Classic and versatile for many product shapes.
- **Drawer (sliding) box.** An inner tray slides out of a sleeve. Creates a reveal moment, well suited to smaller items such as accessories or cards.
- **Book-style box.** Opens like a book; the inside cover can carry a printed message from your company.
- **Collapsible rigid box.** Ships flat and is assembled at the destination, saving freight volume. Useful when shipping large quantities internationally.

## Inserts: holding everything in place

A custom insert keeps each item secure and presents the set the way you intended. Options include:

- **EVA or foam** for precise, cushioned cut-outs, good for fragile items like glassware or electronics.
- **Paperboard inserts** as a lighter alternative to foam.
- **Molded pulp** as a paper-based insert option with a natural look.
- **Satin or fabric lining** for a premium finish.

Send samples of the actual products if possible; inserts made from real samples fit far better than ones made from drawings alone.

## Branding and finishes

This is where the box becomes yours.

- **Foil stamping** in gold, silver or other colors for logos and accents.
- **Embossing or debossing** for a raised or pressed logo you can feel.
- **Spot UV** for a glossy highlight on a matte surface.
- **Specialty papers** — textured, colored or soft-touch — to set the tone.
- **Printed interiors** for a message, pattern or QR code linking to a greeting video.
- **Ribbons and pull tabs** for an easy, gift-like opening.

A restrained design with one or two premium finishes often looks more refined than many effects combined. For luxury and wine-related sets, PackJoyU's dark-and-gold styling is a natural fit.

## Building a realistic timeline

Corporate gifting is usually tied to a fixed date: year-end holidays, New Year, a company anniversary, a product launch or a trade show. Plan backwards from the delivery date and include:

## Shipping corporate gifts

If gifts are sent to individual recipients, the rigid box usually needs an outer corrugated shipping carton to protect it from courier handling. A branded mailer as the outer layer extends the experience from the doorstep. For bulk delivery to offices or events, boxes can be packed in master cartons on pallets. Discuss export packing and documentation with the manufacturer; trade terms are agreed in the quote.

## Sustainability considerations

Many companies want their gifts to reflect their environmental commitments. Options include paperboard or molded pulp inserts instead of foam, recycled or responsibly sourced papers, and designs that encourage reuse. ## Budget drivers

Without listing prices, the factors that move cost most are: box size and style, wrapping paper, number of finishes, insert material and complexity, quantity, and shipping method (assembled versus flat). Requesting quotes for two or three configurations helps you choose the best value for your program.

## Summary

Good corporate gift packaging starts with a clear brief, a box style suited to the items and delivery method, inserts made from real samples, and a timeline that leaves room for sampling and shipping. Get these right and the box becomes part of the gift.
